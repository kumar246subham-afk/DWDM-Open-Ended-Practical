1. Introduction to 5-Number Summary in Data Exploration

In Data Warehousing and Data Mining (DWDM), exploratory data analysis (EDA) demands robust descriptive statistics capable of summarizing large-scale numeric distributions without succumbing to distortions caused by extreme values (outliers). The Five-Number Summary is an indispensable non-parametric statistical summary representing data location, spread, and skewness through five order statistics:

1. Minimum (Min) & Maximum (Max) Identifies the absolute boundary extremes:
Min = x(1),   Max = x(N)
Represents lowest and highest transaction prices within the temporal window.
2. Median (Q2 / 50th Percentile) Measures central tendency invariant to skewness:
Median = x((N+1)/2) (N odd) or [x(N/2) + x(N/2+1)]/2 (N even)
Reflects true equilibrium without outlier distortion.
3. First Quartile (Q1 / 25th Percentile) Delimits the bottom 25% of the sorted observations:
Q1 = Value at rank index 0.25 × (N + 1)
Serves as the lower boundary of the candle body.
4. Third Quartile (Q3 / 75th Percentile) Delimits the bottom 75% (top 25%) of the sorted observations:
Q3 = Value at rank index 0.75 × (N + 1)
Serves as the upper boundary of the candle body.

2. Dispersion Metrics & Outlier Boundaries

Metric Formula Analytical Significance in Stock Market Mining
Interquartile Range (IQR) IQR = Q3 - Q1 Measures core volatility (middle 50% price dispersion). Unaffected by transient spikes.
Lower Outlier Fence LF = Q1 - 1.5 × IQR Identifies anomalous downward crashes, flash sales, or fat-finger trading errors.
Upper Outlier Fence UF = Q3 + 1.5 × IQR Identifies anomalous speculative upward surges or abrupt corporate announcement spikes.
Bowley Skewness (Sk) Sk = [(Q3 - Q2) - (Q2 - Q1)] / [Q3 - Q1] Measures directional asymmetry: Sk > 0 indicates bullish tail; Sk < 0 indicates bearish tail.

3. Structural Mapping: 5-Number Summary vs. Traditional Candlesticks

Traditional financial candlesticks record only 4 discrete points: Open, High, Low, and Close (OHLC). While standard, OHLC is vulnerable to intra-period noise because Open and Close represent singular boundary snapshots. In this practical, we synthesize Box-Whisker Distribution Candles:

  • Upper Wick / Whisker (Max - Q3): Extends from the 75th percentile to the Maximum price achieved.
  • Real Body (Q1 → Q3): Represents the interquartile body enclosing the middle 50% of all traded prices.
  • Median Divider (Q2): Prominent central line revealing whether price action consolidated higher or lower.
  • Lower Wick / Whisker (Q1 - Min): Extends from the First Quartile down to the Minimum price observed.
  • Color Coding: Green if Close ≥ Open (or Q3 - Q2 > Q2 - Q1), Red if Close < Open.

4. Multi-Timeframe Analytical Framework (Daily, Weekly, Monthly)

In market data mining, temporal aggregation granularities dictate the nature of extractable patterns. Designing candles across three hierarchical time scales enables data scientists to filter out micro-noise and isolate systemic trends:

Timeframe Data Sampling Granularity 5-Number Summary Interpretation Trading & Data Mining Utility
Daily Candle 10 Intraday hourly interval samples (9:15 AM to 3:30 PM IST). Min/Max define intraday extremes; Q1/Q3 capture the high-volume core trading band; Q2 tracks midday equilibrium. Used for intraday momentum, liquidity profiling, and detecting opening/closing price manipulation.
Weekly Candle Daily closing/settlement prices across the 5 consecutive trading days (Mon–Fri). Min/Max capture weekly extremes; IQR represents weekly consolidation volatility; Median exposes true weekly balance. Used for swing trading analysis, weekly support/resistance level identification, and short-term forecasting.
Monthly Candle Weekly settlement benchmarks spanning 4 consecutive calendar trading weeks. Min/Max indicate monthly range; Q1/Q3 isolate monthly institutional accumulation; Q2 reveals fair market value. Macro-trend evaluation, institutional portfolio rebalancing, and long-term trend mining.

6. Data Preprocessing & Algorithmic Pipeline

The extract-transform-load (ETL) pipeline implemented for calculating the five-number summary and rendering the candles follows rigorous data warehouse standards:

  1. Data Extraction: Historical tick and settlement feeds extracted from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) order books for October 2024.
  2. Data Cleaning & Formatting: Removal of non-trading weekend timestamps, adjustment for stock splits/dividends, and normalization of float values to 2 decimal places.
  3. Temporal Window Partitioning: Partitioning observation sets into Daily (Dt), Weekly (Wt), and Monthly (Mt) datasets.
  4. Order Statistics Computation: Sorting arrays in ascending order: x(1) ≤ x(2) ≤ ... ≤ x(N).
  5. Quartile Evaluation: Applying linear interpolation on rank indices to compute Q1, Median, and Q3.
  6. Candlestick Synthesis & Rendering: Generating dual-shadow box structures where body = [Q1, Q3], center mark = Q2, lower wick = [Min, Q1], and upper wick = [Q3, Max].

import numpy as np, pandas as pd, matplotlib.pyplot as plt, matplotlib.patches as patches def build_5num_candle(ax, prices, label, is_bullish=True): arr = np.sort(prices) mn, q1, med, q3, mx = np.min(arr), np.percentile(arr, 25), np.median(arr), np.percentile(arr, 75), np.max(arr) iqr = q3 - q1 ax.plot([1, 1], [mn, mx], color='#212121', lw=1.8) # Whiskers ax.add_patch(patches.Rectangle((0.8, q1), 0.4, iqr, facecolor='#26a69a' if is_bullish else '#ef5350', edgecolor='#000', lw=1.5)) ax.plot([0.8, 1.2], [med, med], color='#FFD600', lw=2.2) # Median Notch return {"Min": mn, "Q1": q1, "Median": med, "Q3": q3, "Max": mx, "IQR": iqr}
